# Changelog

## 0.27.0: Home, and 244 movements

Pahlavan now opens on **Home**: today's session, what needs you, and how you're doing, in plain words, each a click away from the tab with the detail. The coach has 244 movements to choose from (it was 100), each with how to set up, do it and what to avoid, and you can read them all in the new exercise catalogue. 0.26.0 was not released on its own: its changes (the coach designs, the engine checks; muscle regions; findings confirmed before they change the plan; standing rules) arrive with this release, and its notes follow below.

- **Home, a new first tab: the overview in plain words.** Pahlavan now opens on Home, and the other tabs are there when you want the detail. The banner shows today's session, how long it takes, what your morning check-in changes today and any swap, with **Log session** and **See the workout**, beside the week's ring and how much of the week is left. Under it: **Needs you** (up to three things to do or check, each opening the tab that settles it, or "Nothing needs you today"), **Food and habits** (today's protein target and habits), **Progress** (one sentence and three tiles: strength, body weight and showing up, each with a small line of its history), and **Your tabs** (a short guide to the other tabs: what each is for, where it stands now, and a dot where something waits). Home uses no engine terms, band names or legends. After the update Pahlavan opens on Home once; from then on it opens on the tab you used last.
- **Home's look:** the Pahlavan logo at the top of the banner; Strength shows an arrow per lift, Body weight its line over a soft fill, Showing up a bar per week with a small ring; a calm "Nothing needs you today" with a faint girih, where Food and habits takes the extra room; each tab has its own accent colour (on Your tabs and in the tab bar); and on opening, the cards rise in and the rings fill (off with reduced motion).
- **Today's At a glance starts folded,** since Home gives the overview; open it once and it stays open.
- **The exercise library: 244 movements for the coach to choose from** (it was 100). Bodyweight and calisthenics ladders (push-up, pull-up, row, dip, handstand, L-sit, planche, front lever, muscle-up), home and hotel options with dumbbells, a kettlebell or bands, more gym machines, jumps, cardio modes (rowing, the elliptical, stairs, swimming, a jump rope), and mobility, warm-up, cool-down, balance and desk-break drills. Each has guide text (the setup, the movement, the mistake to avoid), its other common names, the tools that can stand in for its own (a kettlebell for a goblet squat's dumbbell), what it asks of the body (weight on the wrists, hanging, lying on the back, jumping: facts for the coach, nothing screens on them yet), its roles and whether it suits a hotel room. `engine catalogue` lists them all, with 20 ladders of catalogue movements beside the self-check's four; `--pattern jump` is new, `--equipment` knows parallel bars ("dip station"), a trap bar ("hex bar"), a sled, a jump rope, a stability ball ("swiss ball"), an ab wheel and a pool, and counts a tool that stands in.
- **A readable exercise catalogue.** Every movement Pahlavan knows, grouped by pattern, each with its muscles, regions, tools, level, easier and harder versions, and its setup, do and avoid, then the ladders and what each demand tag means: `docs/exercise-catalogue.md` on the web, and in your vault through the new command **Pahlavan: Write the exercise catalogue** (`Exercise catalogue.md` in your fitness folder, from your installed version). The coach can print it with `engine catalogue --md`. A test keeps the web copy the same as the engine's.
- **A ladder counts at the level your plan starts it at.** A ladder in `plan-data.json` takes an optional `startLevel` (1-based, as `engine log ladder --level` counts): before any level is logged, its "your level" items count at that level, not the lowest. A coach's draft checked with `check --plan` or `review --plan`, or a new plan before its first logged level, used to read a pull-up ladder as dead hangs, so the lats and biceps came out short. The level is now the log's (a logged level always wins), else the plan's `startLevel`, else the lowest, with a `ladder-start` warning that names how to declare a start. `review`, `check`, the Plan screen and Progress read the same level, and so do the session's list and the effort rule. A `startLevel` that isn't one of the ladder's levels is a hard error in `check`. A plan export writes the self-check's level as each ladder's `startLevel`. Plans without it count as before.
- **The install date, and the beta's promise.** Pahlavan records the day it first runs in a vault (a vault from before this version gets the day it updated) and shows it at the top of its Settings tab: "Beta user since 8 Nov 2026". Pahlavan is free during the beta; 1.0 will be paid, and everyone who installs before 1.0 keeps it free. The date stays in the vault; nothing leaves your Mac. The README says the same.
- **Your numbers may change if your plan names one of these.** A chin-up, a walking lunge, an archer or elevated pike push-up, a weighted pull-up, an assisted pistol, a shrimp squat, a tuck hollow hold, an L-sit, a pike hold on a bench, a wall walk, a handstand kick-up and a freestanding handstand now read as their own movements, not a nearby one; each keeps the muscles it was counted with, so your sets per muscle stay, but its pattern, strain tag and level are its own. A movement Pahlavan didn't know before now counts with the catalogue's tags: a band external rotation counts as pulling (rear-delt work), a burpee counts no sets (conditioning), and `muscle-tags-differ` names a guide entry that disagrees, which an `override` reason keeps as yours.
- **The plan generator and the Hotel version's swaps don't change:** `engine plan` (the optional baseline) and the automatic Hotel swaps still choose from the 100 starter movements. The plan check's region options draw on the whole catalogue, and a confirmed finding leaves out the new overhead presses, loaded hinges and hard single-leg moves as it does the old ones.
- **For developers:** `docs/exercise-library.md` holds the library's method, its fields and conventions, how its tags were set and its open questions; the tests hold its rules (links both ways, levels in order, no circles, the hotel rule, names that are each one movement's own, the 0.26 tag audit).

## 0.26.0: the coach designs, the engine checks

Your coach now designs every plan itself, from your answers and Pahlavan's evidence library, and the engine does the numbers, checks each plan against the safety rules and the evidence, and follows the rules the coach writes. Muscles now have regions where training studies support them, and a finding from your photos or self-check is a question for you before it changes anything.

**Your numbers may change.** Three changes move numbers you already see:

- **Sets per muscle use the engine's muscle tags** for every movement it knows, on every screen and in the coach. Rear-delt work (face pulls, rear-delt flyes, band pull-aparts) now counts upper back first and shoulders second, so your shoulder sets may drop; a lunge no longer counts the glutes twice. Your guide's own tags still count for movements the engine doesn't know, or where an entry gives an `override` reason (`"muscles": {..., "override": "why"}`); a hammer curl and an L-sit pull-up are no longer read as a curl and a pull-up. `review` and `check` warn `muscle-tags-differ` for each guide entry whose copy disagrees, and the next plan export brings the copies in step.
- **Hamstrings are no longer credited from squats, the leg press, bridges and hip thrusts** (training studies show little or no hamstring growth from them), so your hamstring sets may drop. Deep squats and the leg press credit the adductors in the engine's own plan count, and rows, Romanian deadlifts and deadlifts, and pull-ups now help the forearms (grip work, a rough guide).
- **The calibration week now does what it says:** in plan week 1 (and the first week back after 3 or more weeks off) every target holds the last weight at 3 reps in reserve, and nothing proposes an increase until week 2 (`review` gives what progression would have given as `heldProgression`); no stall is called and the e1RM trend starts there.

The changes:

- **The coach designs; the engine calculates and checks.** The coach designs the split, the exercises, the variety, the order, the interval formats and the short days from `engine evidence` (the graded rules) and `engine catalogue` (the movements), compares at least three options, and writes the plan through `engine plan-write`. The plan generator (`engine plan`) is now an optional baseline the coach may compare against, and says so (`generator-baseline`).
- **`engine check`: the guardrails a plan is held to.** Hard errors block a write and nothing clears them: a movement your Ledger or the safety screen rules out (checked on `--date`), data Pahlavan can't read as written, a logged lift with no `lifts` entry. Everything evidence-based is a warning with its rule, grade and reason: sets per muscle against the tiers, a priority muscle's 10–16 (counting direct sets near failure), the weekly and per-session caps, RIR by type of exercise, rep ranges and rest, pull to push under the posture emphasis, a light week in every mesocycle, the profile's session lengths, load jumps, exercises changed inside a mesocycle, items with no "Why:" line, a week-by-week change written in a note, and the region rules below. It exits 1 on a hard error.
- **Overrides with reasons.** A plan may go outside an evidence range on purpose: its `overrides` (`{code, item, reason, decision}`) turn the matching warning into information shown with the reason. An override needs a reason, never clears a hard error, and one that matches nothing is listed.
- **Your own plan rules, checked.** The profile's `planRules` holds the rules you gave for any plan (sets per exercise, what every lifting session holds, core on short days), and `check` says where a plan breaks one.
- **Drafts before they're written.** `review`, `session` and `check` take `--plan <file>`, a draft read exactly as the written plan will be, so its sets per muscle, session lengths and targets are what Pahlavan will show. `engine plan-write --file <draft>` checks it (refusing a hard error), lists its warnings, overrides and what changes key by key, and with `--write` writes the plan and reads it back.
- **The plan's standing rules are the coach's.** The coach writes them into `plan-data.json` with the plan and revises them at the check-in: `rules` (calibration, light and test weeks, what a low-readiness day or a sore muscle changes, missed time, the stall response, Hotel), each logged lift's `progression` (double, linear, hold, or a target of the coach's) and `express.short`. Pahlavan does each rule's arithmetic and starts every target's reason with it ("The plan's rule: …"); a rule the plan doesn't set keeps the old default, said as "Pahlavan's default", with one `legacy-default` note. A plan that sets no rules gets the same numbers as before.
- **Light weeks come from the plan.** Every screen reads one light-week calendar: every 4th week unless `rules.lightWeeks` says otherwise (every N weeks, or the weeks listed, with their sets, load and RIR), and a mesocycle ends at each light week. An early signal (low readiness with a falling e1RM, stalls, poor recovery) is a fact for the coach unless the rule says to act on it; the coach moves a light week by editing the plan. `review` adds `deload.signals`, `week.mesocycleWeeks` and `rules`.
- **A lift's step and its stalls are the coach's.** Under a lift's own rule an increase over the load-jump guide (about 10 % a week, a rough guide) is reported, not capped; a coach-set target holds once met; with `rules.stall.response: "report"` a stalled lift gets the facts (`lifts[].stallFacts`) and the coach picks the response.
- **The Plan screen** gains a **Standing rules** card (each rule in words, marked "default: your coach hasn't set this" until the coach does), and its phase targets are the ones your coach set in the profile.
- **`engine evidence`: the rules as data.** Each rule with its number (read from the constant the engine checks with), its grade, a note and its sources: volume, effort, load, rest, tempo, order, range of motion, progression, light weeks, the calibration week, cardio, activity, the regions, how a finding is confirmed, and each standing rule with its default. `--topic` gives one part; `check`'s grades come from the same rules.
- **`engine catalogue`: search the movements** by pattern, region (by name too, "side delts"; an unsupported one such as "inner chest" searches its nearest option and says why), muscle, the equipment at hand and text, each with its muscles, regions, abdominal-strain tag, level and easier and harder versions. The order is for display, never a choice; the version that loads a two-joint muscle long (the seated leg curl, overhead triceps extensions, standing calf raises) comes first. A movement the catalogue doesn't hold counts like one in it once its guide entry names its muscles.
- **Muscle regions.** The engine knows the parts of a muscle that training studies show growing differently with the exercise: the quads' rectus femoris and vasti, the hamstrings at the hip and at the knee, the gastrocnemius and soleus, the triceps long head, the front and side delts, the upper chest, and as rough guides the rear delts, the upper traps and the mid-back, each with its grade and sources. Catalogue movements say which region they bias, and the profile can declare `priorityRegions`. Popular ideas the evidence doesn't support (the inner chest, the VMO, upper lats, the biceps peak, upper glutes) are warned about with the nearest supported option (`unsupported-region`).
- **Sets per region, and region gaps.** A set counts for each region its movement biases (1 when the region's muscle is a main muscle, half when it helps); your sets per muscle don't change. `review` reports every region planned and done (`volume.regions`). `check` warns `region-coverage` when a priority muscle's region gets under 4 direct sets a week and `region-declared-short` when a region you declared weak gets under 6 near failure, each with up to 5 catalogue movements that fit your equipment, Ledger, dislikes and confirmed findings; both are rough guides an override can answer.
- **Declared weak regions, followed through.** A region you declare weak makes its muscle a priority muscle everywhere: `review`'s bands, `check`'s 10–16, and the Progress and Plan screens (it was a priority for the region rules only). This week and the last 4 weeks now judge a declared region's sets by effort: a set logged at 4 or more reps in reserve, or a ladder level under yours that day, no longer counts toward its 6 near failure, and a set logged without RIR takes the plan item's. A region short of its aim that nothing in the catalogue can cover with your equipment shows "No option" instead of "Under". The options `check` lists leave out what a confirmed finding rules out (no overhead presses for the side delts with a confirmed limited overhead reach). `catalogue --equipment` leaves a word it doesn't know out with a warning instead of stopping. The pec deck is a chest fly, a pattern of its own, so the plan builder never makes it a main lift; the slider leg curl is an intermediate movement (a beginner's plan keeps the glute bridge).
- **Regions on the screens.** On Progress, hovering a muscle splits its sets into direct sets and half credit from helping lifts and lists its regions; a **Regions** list under the body map shows each region with an aim as **Under** or **OK**, your declared ones starred, the unmeasured ones marked "rough guide". The Plan's Sets per muscle gives each declared region its own line ("Side delts: 6 direct, aim 6").
- **Nine more movements, 100 in all:** the lying leg curl, a slider leg curl, the seated calf raise, a band lateral raise, the reverse pec deck, the cable overhead triceps extension, the pec deck, the hip abduction machine and the dumbbell shrug, each with its muscles, region, strain tag, level and easier and harder options. "Lying Leg Curl" in a plan now reads as the lying curl, and their common names are recognised.
- **Findings ask before they act.** A body-assessment row or a self-check result that would change exercise choice (a rib flare on the overhead reach, a rounded hinge, balance under 30 s) is a question for you first, never a diagnosis, and the profile records your answer (`emphasis[].confirmed`, `selfCheck.confirmed.<test>`). Confirmed, it applies as before; declined, only its warm-up lines stay, so overhead presses come back when you press overhead without trouble; not answered yet, it ranks the regression first and rules nothing out. A confirmed lagging area makes its muscle a priority muscle in every count (`check`, `review`, the screens), as it always did in the plan builder. A profile from before has no answers: its plan is unchanged and `plan` and `review` warn `finding-unconfirmed` with the question to ask.
- **Plan items have `rir` and `timed` fields,** read before the note: `rir` a number or `[lo, hi]`, `timed` a timed item's minutes and, for intervals, rounds, work, rest, warm-up and cool-down. Plans without them read as before.
- **Intervals count as intervals.** A timed item's sets are its rounds, so a 22-minute item no longer shows as 11 sets; Today, the Log panel, the Plan screen and a copied plan say the structure in words, and a session's length counts the warm-up, rounds, easy minutes and cool-down. In the calibration week intervals are easy steady minutes, with no week-1 line needed in the note.
- **A plan export keeps your names and reasons.** Exported over your plan file (`plan --format plan-data --vault .`), a movement your plan, your `lifts` or your log already names keeps that name (the catalogue's "One-arm dumbbell row" stays your "One-Arm DB Row"), with its history, lifts entry and guide text; a logged lift keeps its name even when its guide entry keeps its own tags (an override, which then counts: `muscle override` says so); an item unchanged in its session keeps its "Why:" line; every item without one is listed (`why lines`) for your coach to write, and so are the form cues and temporary changes of the notes as written that the export leaves out (`note cues`, `temporary changes`). The export writes the calibration, light and test weeks and each lift's rule as standing rules.
- **Your plan starts on the day you chose.** `plan-data.json` takes an optional `start` (a date): the Monday-to-Sunday week holding it is plan week 1 on every screen and in the coach from the day the plan is written, so the plan's weeks and dates agree from the start (they counted from the current week until a session was logged). The coach writes it with a fresh start's or a reset's plan; the next month, a phase review's next block and every other change keep it, so week 9 follows week 8 (`check` and `plan-write` warn `start-changed` on a draft that drops or moves it, and an export over your plan carries it). `plan --start D` starts the generator on the week holding D and writes it. A plan without one counts as before. Before the start week, Today shows no session and says when your plan starts.
- **A first plan works on a fresh vault.** With no plan written yet (right after the intake), `plan --format plan-data --vault .` exports a fresh plan for the coach to start its drafts from, with a `no-plan` note, and `review --plan`, `session --plan`, `check --plan` and `plan-write` work on the draft. `review`, `readiness`, `session`, `tracked`, `due`, `habits`, `food`, the check-in template and the day answer with a `no-plan` warning instead of stopping; `check` with no plan says there is nothing to check; what needs the plan (picking a session, a lift never logged, a ladder level, a fix) says `no-plan`; `log add` takes a custom session. Without a plan there are no plan weeks, phases or mesocycles.
- **Too many priority muscles, said.** `check` warns `priority-room` (a rough guide) when your priority muscles (the profile's, your declared weak regions' and confirmed lagging areas) are more than your week has room for: 3 with 4 or more lifting days, 2 with 3, 1 with 2 or 30-minute sessions. A priority trained only on the short days doesn't count; one with direct sets on a lifting day does. The plan builder's `priority-over-room` now counts the same muscles the same way. The coach asks which come first and keeps the rest under Later priorities, or keeps them all with an override and its reason; at the Sunday check-in it says so once, then leaves it for the phase review.
- **The coach asks before it proposes.** The plan job asks its open questions in one message (each finding you haven't answered, priority muscles past your week's room, the start date, and "picture week 6: the plan has failed, why?") and waits for your answers before it designs; then the options, the proposal and your yes, then the writes. It runs each engine command on its own, so you see every one, and reads the evidence library by topic.
- **Smaller fixes.** The Plan screen names the Ledger's ruled-out movements and marks one Pahlavan doesn't recognise; a session logged without its exercise list is counted from today's plan and marked approximate (`snapshot-rebuilt`); engine text names its own `log ladder` command, never a script only one vault had; the generator meets each major muscle's minimum of 4 sets when it can.
- **For developers:** `tools/eval-plans.mjs` scores coach-written draft plans for the made-up test athletes (hard errors, warnings, overrides, priority muscles in band, region rules met, session minutes, pull to push), so models and prompts can be compared. `engine --version --json` gives `facts` (the calibration RIR, the volume tiers, the default light week, the region rules, plan-data's keys and every `unexpressible` kind) from the engine's constants; the skill's self-check fails a ship whose docs contradict them, quote a field as missing that exists, keep a Python snippet the engine replaced, or whose engine output names a vault-only script, and `npm run ship-skill` runs `engine check` on the test vault first. The self-check passes on a vault with no plan yet: its draft runs read a fresh export, and the commands that need the plan are held to their documented `no-plan` error. `tools/terminal-svg.mjs --transcript` reads a Claude Code session file (`.jsonl`) as JSON, counting only the main session's assistant text (not a subagent's), so a coach line with quotes checks against it; a `.json` export holding the events is read the same way and any other JSON is refused with a message; a plain-text transcript works as before.

## 0.25.2: Obsidian 1.7.2 or later

- **Needs Obsidian 1.7.2 or later** (it was 1.5.0). Pahlavan opens its tab with Obsidian's `revealLeaf`, which since 1.7.2 waits for the tab to load; the directory's review asks the declared minimum to cover it. Obsidian 1.7.2 came out in October 2024.

## 0.25.1: Obsidian's review

Changes from the community directory's automated review. Nothing you do in Pahlavan changes.

- **Popout windows:** the screens are built on the window in use (Obsidian's `activeDocument`) and their timers run on it, as Obsidian's guidelines ask.
- **The open command** is now **Pahlavan: Open** in the command palette (it was "Pahlavan: Open Pahlavan").
- **For developers:** `npm run build` builds the plugin, the same on any system (the engine alone is `npm run build-engine`); `package-lock.json` lists every dependency, so `npm ci` works; the preview builds from the public repository; the code passes Obsidian's lint rules, with two exceptions: the coach's command-line engine runs outside Obsidian, where `Vault#configDir` doesn't exist, and the settings don't yet use Obsidian 1.13's settings search.

## 0.25.0: the first public beta

The first public version of Pahlavan, free while it's in beta. It was built and used in private before this, from 0.1 to 0.24.

- **The plugin:** Today (the session, how to do each movement and why it's in your plan, a checklist, the log), Progress (your week, strength over time, the body record and photos, your habits with 4-week charts for sitting, stand-ups and desk breaks), Plan, Check-in, Food, and reminder cards with quiet hours.
- **The coach:** the `/fitness-coach` skill for Claude Code (intake, plan, logging, the Sunday check-in and letter, reviews, progress reports, an eating plan, habits), set up from Pahlavan's welcome card or its settings.
- **Licence:** the Elastic License 2.0. The code is open to read, use and change; it can't be offered as a service, have a licence key removed or got around, or lose its notices.
- **Calls are not detected:** a stand-up or sitting count treats a call without typing as time away. Pausing during calls, through a small helper program you choose in the settings, is planned for a later version.
