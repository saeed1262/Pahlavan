# Changelog

## 0.28.1: a short day after two lifting days, and fixes

- **No third lifting day in a row.** Today picked lifting or short by the weekday alone, so a week that slipped a day could stack three lifting days: lift on Tuesday and Wednesday (Wednesday's short day), and Thursday still offered the next lift. Now a lifting day after two logged lifting days in a row offers the next short session instead, while the week still has short sessions to do, and says why ("you lifted on Tuesday and Wednesday"). Once the week's short sessions are done, the lift stays. A rest day, an unlogged day or a custom session doesn't count as lifting, so a week on schedule reads as before. The reminders, the Food tab's kind of day, the gym page and the phone follow the same rule, and **Change session** still offers the lift.
- **Answer on a snoozed card.** Due today keeps a snoozed card's row ("Did you train today?") with its **Answer** button, but Answer said the card wasn't due. Answer now opens it, and an answer closes it as before; on their own, cards still wait out the snooze.
- **Code cleanup for the directory's review.** The changes Obsidian's automated review of 0.28.0 asked for: no inline styles in the Try it demo, values typed where they were read as `any`, unneeded type assertions and unused names removed. Nothing you see changes.

## 0.28.0: Movements, every set, and plans from a blank page

A new **Movements** tab to search every movement with its guide, weight targets on the weights your gym actually has, every set logged with one tap, a coach that counts each exercise's skips, swaps and pain and decides your priority muscles from your assessment, HRV and an "I'm ill today" answer in the morning check-in, a gym page for logging every set from your phone, your coach's reasons for your plan on the Plan tab, and the old plan generator gone. You can try it all in your browser before you install it.

When you ask for a new plan, your coach now designs it from a blank page, from your answers and the evidence, and sets it beside the plan you're on only after it has picked one: what's kept, what changes, and which of your logged lifts stay, which you choose. At each review it plans the next block like a coach, from the long-term plan and what the last block's data shows, keeping a movement only when the data earns it. Every plan check also says when a movement needs equipment you don't have or is one you said you dislike. The rest are fixes (the plan's starting ladder level on the screens, intervals counted as hard work, the Finisher tag, photos in the plan's own test weeks, the coach's version checked both ways), the L-sit pull-up, and an engine command for the eating plan's numbers.

- **Try it in your browser.** A demo page with the real screens on a made-up athlete, with nothing to install: every tab, the Log panel and the settings work, and what you change stays in the page until you reload. It loads nothing from anywhere and sends nothing (its own content security policy refuses every request). Light or dark, or the Isfahan look. Linked from the README and the user guide.
- **Movements, a seventh tab after Food.** Search as you type: a name, another name for it, a muscle or a tool ("row", "glutes", "band"). Filter by group (rows, squats, mobility and the rest), by muscle, by level, and with **My equipment** to only what your profile's equipment covers (a tool that stands in counts). The results come in groups. Each movement shows its level, the tools it needs (with "Needs rings" when your equipment doesn't cover it), what it works, and **In your plan** when one of your sessions trains it. Its name opens the movement guide: the body map, the setup, the movement, the mistake to avoid and a video search. Its easier and harder steps are links: a click clears the filters, finds that movement and opens its guide. Beside the list are the ladders: your plan's own with the level you're on, the four the self-check starts, and the catalogue's, each step a click away. A self-check ladder your plan already has shows once, as yours, and a second ladder of the same name is titled by its range ("Pull-up: dead hang to weighted pull-up"). Without a plan or a profile the tab still shows the whole catalogue. The tab saves nothing and makes no network requests: a video link opens your browser only when you click it.
- **Two ways in:** the command **Pahlavan: Browse movements**, and **Browse movements** in the Plan tab's notes card.
- **Seven tabs in a narrower pane:** the date and the **?** move under the tab bar when they don't fit beside it, and in a narrow pane the tab bar wraps, so no tab runs off the side.
- **Targets on your gym's real weights.** The profile can list the weights your gym has (`profile.json` `loads`, in kg or lb): dumbbells and kettlebells as a range with its step or as a list, the bar and its plates, a trap bar, and the machine and cable steps. A lift's kind of kit is its catalogue movement's equipment (or dumbbells for a lift marked `db`). An increase goes to the nearest real weight above the last one (20 kg dumbbells in 2 kg steps go to 22, not 21 or 22.5; a 60 kg barbell with plates down to 1.25 kg goes to 62.5), and a lighter target (a light week, two misses, a stall reset, the first week back) to the nearest one below (a light week from 22 kg is 20). The load-jump cap counts one real step as one step, and when even the next real weight is over it, the weight holds and the target asks for one more rep, with the reason in its note. A last weight heavier than the kit's heaviest (one logged at another gym) is kept, with a note to correct the loads, and at the lightest weight two misses hold it and a stall gets no reset, each saying there is nothing lighter. A set logged in the other unit counts as the real weight nearest it, and a ladder slot moves to its next variation, never onto the kit. Steps and plates are at least 0.25 kg (0.5 lb), loads at most 500 kg (1100 lb) and a dumbbell range or list at most 200 weights. Today, the session, the letter and `next` all follow, and the Log panel's weight stepper walks the real weights. Without `loads`, nothing changes but the fix below.
- **At the heaviest weight, reps carry it.** At the heaviest dumbbell (or kettlebell, or the top of a stack) the weight holds and the target asks for one more rep than the last session's fewest, under the title Load ceiling: "the heaviest dumbbell here is 30 kg, so reps carry it". The training rules' load ceiling was advice for the coach; the engine now applies it.
- **The coach asks for the weights.** The intake's equipment question asks for them in plain words, and a plan run with none recorded asks once. `check` warns `step-not-in-gym` (a fact, no grade) for a lift whose step the kit can't make, with the step to set: a 1 kg step on 2 kg dumbbells, or a 1.25 kg step on a bar whose smallest pair of plates is 2.5 kg. Loads in the other unit than a lift are converted for it (to 0.1), with a `loads-unit-differs` warning.
- **A 1.25 step stays exact.** An increase rounded the last weight plus the step to 0.5, as the old dashboard did, so a 1.25 kg step turned 60 kg into 61.5. Increases now round to the finer of 0.5 and the step: 60 + 1.25 is 61.25. Steps of 0.5 and up (2.5, 5, 10) give the same numbers as before.
- **`engine plan` and the plan generator are gone.** Since 0.26 your coach designs every plan, and the generator was only an optional baseline beside it; since 0.28.0 every new plan starts from a blank one (`engine draft`). So the generator, its export to `plan-data.json` and the `engine plan` command are removed, and `engine plan` now stops with "`plan` was removed in 0.28.1: a new plan starts from `engine draft`". Your plan and your numbers don't change. The warnings only the generator gave (`generator-baseline`, `history-not-used`, `priority-over-room`, `priority-short`) go with it; `check`'s `priority-room` still says when your priority muscles are more than your week has room for. On a vault with no plan yet, the coach's self-check runs its plan checks on a small made-up sample plan it ships (`scripts/sample-plan.json`).
- **Why a swap was made, saved with it.** The Log panel now saves each swap's reason with the session: your own swap, a Ledger limit, soreness or the Hotel version. An edit shows it again, and the coach's `engine log add` takes it too. Sessions logged before this version have no reason saved and read as before.
- **Your coach counts skips and swaps.** The coach's review now has the facts for each exercise in your plan, since the cycle start and over the last 4 weeks: how often it was planned, done as planned, skipped (with your reasons) and swapped (with the reasons and what you did instead), and the last day you did it. A swap counts for the exercise it replaced, and the items Express leaves out don't count as planned that day. An older session with no checklist counts as not recorded, never as done. The coach reads these facts when it decides which exercises to keep or change at a review, and quotes them when it proposes a swap at the Sunday check-in.
- **Progress's PR marks after a calibration week.** When a lift's trend starts at a calibration week (or a stall reset) and has a kg/lb mix-up in it, the strength chart now leaves out exactly the sets before the mix-up, as the trend does; it counted them from the wrong place, so an old outlier could hide a PR.
- **When each lift last rose.** The review gives each lift's last rise this cycle: the day of its last PR (the latest e1RM above every earlier one since the cycle start, the one Progress marks), with the sessions and days since, and the best before the cycle beside it.
- **Pain by exercise, and a pain without a number.** The review groups the cycle's pain by exercise (by area when none is named, as with the check-in's aches): how many reports, the highest and the latest, whether it's rising, falling or steady, the pain rule's verdict and the skips for pain. In the Log panel the pain slider now starts unset: if you don't move it or tap it, the pain is saved with no severity ("not given"), not as 3, and the coach asks how bad it was. A tap or click on the slider where it starts (or Enter or Space on it) records that 3. The engine reads pain from the pain field only, never from your notes.
- **Every set, one tap a set.** In the Log panel's **Every set**, each set's number is its tick: tap it once the set is done as shown. Tapping its RIR ticks it too, and so does changing its weight or reps. Only ticked sets are saved. **Every set as the target** fills in and ticks them all, and **Tick every set** does the same where there's no target. The RIR chips are now 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and **5+** (a set too light to judge; 4 was 4+). **What will be saved** says "Goblet Squat: 2 of 3 sets ticked", or "no sets ticked" for an exercise done with none (saved as done, without sets), and the panel's foot counts the sets ticked.
- **Sets start from something real.** Each exercise's sets start from its target, else from last time's sets (every set when you logged every set, else last time's top set on each), and none is ticked. Accessories show **Last** too ("Last 20 kg × 10 · Mon 6 Apr"). A swap starts its sets blank, and nothing from before the cycle start is used.
- **Supersets at Every set.** Each part of a pair (Face Pull + Band External Rotation) gets its own sets, saved as its own exercise. Standard stays as before. A part swapped by the Hotel version (a reverse fly for the face pull) is saved in the session's swaps with its reason, at either level.
- **An edit keeps a swapped exercise's sets.** Editing an Every-set session showed a swapped exercise under the plan's name with no sets, and saving it dropped them and the swap, when the swap was named only beside the sets (a superset part's, or one the coach logged). The edit now shows the exercise done, its sets and the swap, and saving with no change gives back the same entry. `log add` also saves such a swap in `swaps`.
- **The tracking a new log starts at.** Every set when your profile's tracking is `full`; otherwise the one you chose last on this Mac.
- **The weight goes up only when every set of the day reaches the top.** At Every set, fewer sets logged than the day called for hold the weight at the top of the range, under the title Sets not logged: "2 of 3 sets logged: the weight goes up when every set reaches the top." The day's count is the session's own: on a trimmed day (low readiness or sore muscles: one set fewer) or in Express, logging every set it asked for at the top earns the increase. Each new session saves that count beside the plan's (`daySets` in its exercise list). An older session, saved before the count was, is judged as before, on the sets it logged: the panel showed it one row per set of the day. A ladder keeps its variation, and a coach's target isn't met until every set reaches it. With every set logged nothing changes, and Standard tracking is as before. **Your targets may change** if you logged fewer sets than planned at Every set.
- **The coach logs every set too.** `engine log add` now documents and checks `"trackingLevel": "full"` with `exerciseSets`: each name an item of the session (a swap names the plan's item as `planned`), reps whole numbers from 0, weights from 0, RIR 0 to 10, and a logged lift with a top set has its sets there. Anything else stops with exit 1, and nothing is written.
- **HRV in the morning check-in.** The Check-in screen and the morning card have an **HRV** field: heart rate variability in ms, a whole number from 5 to 300 (in Apple Health: Heart, Heart Rate Variability). It's optional, so leave it empty if your watch doesn't measure it. It joins readiness as a sixth input against your own usual, compared on its log because HRV spreads more the higher it is, and Today shows its row beside the others. Without HRV, nothing changes.
- **The HRV note.** When your HRV is 20% or more under your own 4-week usual two mornings running, Today and the coach's review say so. On its own it's a note and changes nothing. Hard training stops only when the resting-HR flag or a Low score comes with it. The note needs 14 mornings with HRV first, and until then the review says the baseline is building.
- **Watch numbers alone don't score a morning.** A morning gets a readiness score only with at least one of your own answers (energy, soreness or sleep quality) among its scored inputs. Before HRV, numbers alone could never reach the three inputs a score needs; sleep hours, resting HR and HRV now could, so the rule is written down. No past morning's score changes.
- **"I'm ill today".** The morning check-in and the morning card have an **I'm ill today** answer: one tap on a sick day saves it for that day, and readiness stops hard training (easy Zone 2 or mobility at most), as the coach's sick rule always said. A second tap takes it back.
- **A sick day in the files is read.** Readiness never read `illness` from body-data.json, so "no hard training when ill" could not fire from a saved day. It reads it now.
- **Readiness in the evidence library.** `engine evidence --topic readiness` gives the readiness rules with their numbers: how a morning is scored, the bands, the resting-HR flag, short sleep and the HRV note. Each is graded H, with its sources; the HRV studies are in endurance athletes with rMSSD, while Apple Health reports SDNN, so the 20% line is a rough guide.
- **Your coach decides your priority muscles.** The intake used to ask you to pick them, and the plan followed your picks. Now it asks what you care about most (a muscle, a look, something your sport needs) as your wish, and after the photos and the self-check the coach decides the priorities the way a coach would: from the findings you confirmed, your posture and self-check results, your Ledger, your goals in their order and your wish. Each comes with a one-line reason that names its evidence, the coach says where your wish landed and why (kept, ranked lower or waiting for a later phase; one the evidence doesn't support is explained, never quietly dropped), and you confirm or change the list. Your profile keeps each priority with its reason and the date decided, beside your wish and its date. A new plan and each phase review decide them again from your progress, each change with its reason. If your priorities were your own picks, the next plan run decides them this way, with your picks read as your wish.
- **The gym page: every set from your phone.** A page for your phone's browser logs a gym session set by set, without Obsidian on the phone. The Pahlavan shortcut's new first item, **Gym**, opens it on today's session: each exercise with its sets filled in from the target or last time, the rest, a superset's parts and the day's swaps. One tap ticks a set, **edit** changes it (the sets after it follow), a tick starts the rest timer, and **Skip** keeps the reason. **Send to Pahlavan** hands it to the shortcut, which asks before it saves one entry to the phone's inbox, and Pahlavan imports it as an **Every set** session. The page keeps its progress if Safari closes, and sends nothing anywhere itself. **Gym** copies the day's sessions and opens the page, and one tap on **Load today's session** reads them from the clipboard (the phone asks to allow the paste): the shortcut's Open URLs hands the browser only the address of a link that long, never its `#` part, even at 1,300 characters. The page keeps the sessions, so a reload opens on them; a link with its data after the `#` still opens directly.
- **The phone's session entry takes every set.** A session from the phone can now carry every set (`trackingLevel: "full"` with `exerciseSets`), the lines skipped and why, a swap and the cardio minutes. It's checked exactly as the coach's `engine log add` checks a session, and saved as it saves one, Today's checklist included. An entry without them imports as before.
- **Skip reasons for `log add`.** `engine log add` takes `skipWhy` (`{"Zone 2 finisher": "time"}`: time, pain, equipment or energy) beside `skipped`, saved in the checklist as the Log panel saves a reason.
- **Express's finish by its sets.** When Express ends on an exercise (a face pull, 2 × 15) rather than cardio, the Log panel now shows its sets to tick at **Every set** and saves them; it showed a cardio line with no sets, and gave the day cardio minutes.
- **Why this plan, on the Plan tab.** Under the timeline, a new card gives your coach's reasons for your plan, written to you when the plan is written: the plan in a sentence or three, each reason with how strong its evidence is (no tag on a reason that is your own, such as a rule you set or something you asked for), the split your coach picked and why, the others it built and measured and why each lost, and, folded, **Why not the other N splits**, with links to the roadmap and the decision behind the plan. A plan written before this version has no reasons yet: the card says so and gives the command, `/fitness-coach plan why`, with Copy. The coach then writes them from your roadmap, the plan's decision and your profile, shows you the text first, and changes nothing else in your plan. A swap or the next month keeps the card, rewriting only a reason the change touches, and each new plan and each phase gets its own.
- **The splits your coach didn't build, said.** The coach built and compared three ways of laying out your week, but never said why it left the other kinds out. Now each kind of split it didn't build gets one line on why it doesn't fit you (push/pull/legs on 4 days: each muscle gets one hard day a week, and a skipped day loses its whole week), in the proposal, the roadmap, the plan's decision and the Why this plan card.

- **A new plan starts blank.** A redesign (a new plan in place of the one you're on: a reset, or "this plan has gone stale") used to come out as an edit of the old plan: each option was built from the plan as written, which kept its names and "Why:" lines, and the check warned about every exercise it changed, so the option with the fewest warnings was the old plan. Now each of the coach's options starts from `engine draft`, a blank plan in plan-data's key order that carries only the start, the lift unit and the desk breaks from the plan as written, and the coach reads the plan as written only after the pick. For a reset or a redesign, step A's questions ask what you want to keep, what you want changed, what's boring or not working and what has changed for you, and the profile records your answers with the date asked. Your preferences are what you said, never the old plan's split or exercises read as a preference. A phase review's next phase starts from a blank draft too, with the movements its data keeps (below).
- **Reconcile: your current plan, after the pick.** A new step in the coach's plan job sets the pick beside the plan as written: how much it keeps (the share of the new plan's movements also in the old one, and of the old plan's that the new one keeps); each logged lift the pick drops or could keep, with its trade-off (its sessions, e1RM trend and next target against what the pick's choice gives instead), for you to choose in the proposal; and, for a redesign in the middle of a block, the coach's choice between keeping the calendar and starting a new block (its light weeks listed in `rules.lightWeeks.weeks`), with its reason. A logged lift you keep keeps its logged name, so its history still matches. The proposal says what changes from your current plan and what's kept, and the Decision records it.
- **The next phase, planned like a coach.** At a phase review the coach first reads what the roadmap's next phase is for (its aim, emphasis and targets, and where the macrocycle is heading) and what the last phase's numbers show (each lift's e1RM trend and stalls, sets planned against done, the regions, your weight trend against your goal's band, sessions done against planned, readiness, skipped exercises, pain from the log and your check-ins, each target against its prediction), with your answers on what to keep and what to change. Then it gives each current movement a verdict, keep (still progressing, liked, serving the next phase) or change (stalled, often skipped or swapped, painful, boring, or off the next phase's aim), with its reason from that data, and designs its options from a blank draft with the kept movements in each. The proposal opens with "What the last phase taught us" and "How the next phase serves the long-term plan", and the Decision records the verdicts. A phase review keeps the calendar and is checked without `--redesign`, so a high share kept is answered by the verdicts, not an override. Each mesocycle review does the same, smaller: every accessory is kept or changed for its own reason from the block's data, and a main lift changes inside a phase only for pain or a stall the triage ladder hasn't fixed, with an override giving that reason. The old rules (main lifts kept for a phase, at most half the accessories rotating) are now defaults that hold where the data says nothing.
- **How much a plan keeps: `engine overlap` and `--redesign`.** `engine overlap --plans a.json,b.json` gives the share of each plan's movements the other holds, both ways (a movement is its ladder, else its catalogue entry, else its name; timed items and cardio left out), and with `--vault` each plan against the plan as written; `similar` lists the shares over half. The coach runs it on its three options, and two that share over half are one plan twice: it changes one, or says why the overlap stays. `check --plan <draft> --redesign` and `plan-write --redesign` turn the exercise changes into information (`redesign-changes`, in place of `exercise-change` warnings) and warn `similar-to-current` when over half of the draft's movements are in the plan as written (a rough guide), with the shared movements as its items, so an override can answer one or all of them. Nothing is stored in the plan.
- **Fit checks on every `check`.** Two warnings state facts from your profile, with no evidence grade: `equipment-missing` (a catalogue movement your equipment doesn't cover; a tool that stands in counts, bodyweight always does, and cardio is left out) and `disliked-movement` (a movement on your dislikes). The coach swaps the movement, adds the tool to your profile, or keeps it with an override and its reason. A plan you already have may show them at its next check.
- **The plan's starting ladder level on the screens.** Since 0.27.0 a ladder counts at the level your plan starts it at, but the screens still showed it as not set until you logged a level. Progress, Skills now shows the start ("Level 6 of 8 · Pull-up · the plan's start, not logged yet") with **Log this level**, which writes it as your first logged level (until then, ▲ and ▼ move from it); the movement guide marks the plan's starting level, and Today's copied plan lists it under Your levels. `review`'s ladders say where each level comes from (`from`: `log`, `plan` or `lowest`).
- **Intervals count as hard work** in the check for cardio beside lifting. The week as written read every cardio item as easy, and a timed item the guide doesn't name as never hard, so intervals the day before a heavy lower-body day, which the training rules advise against, were never flagged. Now an item whose structure is intervals (two or more timed rounds, from its `timed` field or its note), a lifting day's finisher included, is hard conditioning that loads the legs. Steady cardio stays easy, and so does the calibration week, whose intervals are already easy steady minutes. **Your flags may change:** a plan with intervals the day before a heavy leg day now gets the flag in `review`, which the coach reads at the check-in and when it designs a plan.
- **The Finisher tag, only on a session's finisher.** The Log panel tagged every cardio row **Finisher**, so a short day's cardio, or the only item of an intervals day, read as a finisher, and a short day whose only item is cardio showed it on Today's finisher line. Now the finisher is the cardio that ends a session after its other work (a lifting day's easy cardio, a short day's cool-down after its circuit, Express's own finish), one rule for Today and the Log panel, in a new log and in an edit. A short day of cardio alone shows it as the day's item. The checklist ticks and the cardio minutes are as before.
- **Photos follow the plan's own test weeks.** When the plan lists its test weeks (`rules.tests`), a photo set now comes on each test week's Sunday as well as each light week's (one photo day when they fall together). With the default tests at each phase's end nothing changes, and no light-week photo day goes away. The body record's dates, the photo and test cards, Check-in's Body record, the Plan's photo days, `engine tracked` and `due`, and the baseline note's first photo set follow. Your routine gets a **Test week** row (the tests on Saturday, the photos on Sunday) and no longer puts listed tests on the phase review's Saturday, and the Fitness tests card names a listed test week by its plan week ("Plan week 6 · the plan's own test week"), not "End of <phase> · in the light week, so you test fresh".
- **The coach's version, checked both ways.** Pahlavan only checked whether the coach in your vault matched the copy it carries, so on a vault two Macs share, **Update the coach** could write an older Pahlavan's coach over the newer one the other Mac had set up. It now compares the versions: a newer coach gets no button in the settings, and the command and the write itself refuse, saying to update Pahlavan from Community plugins (the welcome card counts it as set up). At the same version, files that differ are taken as your own edits, and only the settings offer to put them back (**Restore its files**). Home's **Needs you** says which to update (**Update the coach** or **Update Pahlavan**, each opening Pahlavan's settings), and the coach's `review` and `check` warn `version-differs` with both versions and what to do, never blocking a plan write.
- **`engine energy`: the eating plan's numbers from the engine,** in place of the Python snippet the coach ran. From your profile, the trend weight and the eating plan's level and meals: resting energy (Mifflin-St Jeor), maintenance at the activity factor the coach picks (`--pal`, required, inside the diet guide's table), and the goal energy, for a weekly rate (`--rate`, optional) or, without one, your goal's range (recomposition: maintenance to 250 kcal under it; a gain: 200 to 300 kcal over it; fat loss and the other goals: what the goal band gives). Either is held to the safety floor (1,500 kcal a day for men, 1,200 for women; under it, the floor and a slower rate) and never loses faster than 1 % of body weight a week (a faster `--rate` is refused). Then protein a day and a meal, with the top-up that reaches the floor, and the fat and fiber minimums, each figure with its reason and as `eating-plan.json` keeps them. `--meals` defaults to the eating plan's, else 3. With no sex in the profile it uses the female figures, and a warning says so, as the snippet did.
- **The L-sit pull-up joins the catalogue,** now 245 movements. It was only a level of the pull-up ladder, so a plan that named it had no catalogue match: no movement pattern, no abdominal-strain tag, only its guide entry's muscles. Now it's a vertical pull on the bar, advanced, the lats leading with the biceps, upper back, forearms and abs helping (the abs hold the legs level), with moderate abdominal strain and the pull-up and the L-sit as its easier steps. **Your numbers may change if your plan names it:** it counts with the catalogue's tags, and `muscle-tags-differ` names a guide entry that disagrees (an `override` reason keeps yours). The ladder still counts the level as a pull-up, and generated plans don't change.
- **For developers:** `tools/eval-plans.mjs` scores redesigns. An athlete fixture's `input.current` (its plan as written) makes its drafts redesigns, checked with `--redesign`, with the share each keeps (`in current`, `overlap.current` in the JSON); each row counts the fit findings (`time-over`, `ledger-blocked`, `equipment-missing`, `disliked-movement`); and a diversity section under the rows gives the mean and largest share across athletes and every pair of each athlete's candidates, naming the pairs over half (`diversity` in `--json`). `2-desk-recomp` has a current plan and two sample redesigns. `engine --version --json`'s `facts` gain `redesign.overlapWarnAbove` and `energy` (the Mifflin-St Jeor constants, the activity factors, the floors, the 1 % limit, the goal offsets, protein a meal, fat and fiber); the skill's self-check holds the docs' figures to them and runs `draft` and `overlap`, and the one Python snippet left is the water target's. A test keeps `manifest.json`'s version equal to `package.json`'s.

## 0.26.0: the coach designs, the engine checks

Your coach now designs every plan itself, from your answers and Pahlavan's evidence library, and the engine does the numbers, checks each plan against the safety rules and the evidence, and follows the rules the coach writes. Muscles now have regions where training studies support them, and a finding from your photos or self-check is a question for you before it changes anything.

**Your numbers may change.** Three changes move numbers you already see:

- **Sets per muscle use the engine's muscle tags** for every movement it knows, on every screen and in the coach. Rear-delt work (face pulls, rear-delt flyes, band pull-aparts) now counts upper back first and shoulders second, so your shoulder sets may drop; a lunge no longer counts the glutes twice. Your guide's own tags still count for movements the engine doesn't know, or where an entry gives an `override` reason (`"muscles": {..., "override": "why"}`); a hammer curl and an L-sit pull-up are no longer read as a curl and a pull-up. `review` and `check` warn `muscle-tags-differ` for each guide entry whose copy disagrees, and the next plan export brings the copies in step.
- **Hamstrings are no longer credited from squats, the leg press, bridges and hip thrusts** (training studies show little or no hamstring growth from them), so your hamstring sets may drop. Deep squats and the leg press credit the adductors in the engine's own plan count, and rows, Romanian deadlifts and deadlifts, and pull-ups now help the forearms (grip work, a rough guide).
- **The calibration week now does what it says:** in plan week 1 (and the first week back after 3 or more weeks off) every target holds the last weight at 3 reps in reserve, and nothing proposes an increase until week 2 (`review` gives what progression would have given as `heldProgression`); no stall is called and the e1RM trend starts there.

The changes:

- **The coach designs; the engine calculates and checks.** The coach designs the split, the exercises, the variety, the order, the interval formats and the short days from `engine evidence` (the graded rules) and `engine catalogue` (the movements), compares at least three options, and writes the plan through `engine plan-write`. The plan generator (`engine plan`) is now an optional baseline the coach may compare against, and says so (`generator-baseline`).
- **`engine check`: the guardrails a plan is held to.** Hard errors block a write and nothing clears them: a movement your Ledger or the safety screen rules out (checked on `--date`), data Pahlavan can't read as written, a logged lift with no `lifts` entry. Everything evidence-based is a warning with its rule, grade and reason: sets per muscle against the tiers, a priority muscle's 10–16 (counting direct sets near failure), the weekly and per-session caps, RIR by type of exercise, rep ranges and rest, pull to push under the posture emphasis, a light week in every mesocycle, the profile's session lengths, load jumps, exercises changed inside a mesocycle, items with no "Why:" line, a week-by-week change written in a note, and the region rules below. It exits 1 on a hard error.
- **Overrides with reasons.** A plan may go outside an evidence range on purpose: its `overrides` (`{code, item, reason, decision}`) turn the matching warning into information shown with the reason. An override needs a reason, never clears a hard error, and one that matches nothing is listed.
- **Your own plan rules, checked.** The profile's `planRules` holds the rules you gave for any plan (sets per exercise, what every lifting session holds, core on short days), and `check` says where a plan breaks one.
- **Drafts before they're written.** `review`, `session` and `check` take `--plan <file>`, a draft read exactly as the written plan will be, so its sets per muscle, session lengths and targets are what Pahlavan will show. `engine plan-write --file <draft>` checks it (refusing a hard error), lists its warnings, overrides and what changes key by key, and with `--write` writes the plan and reads it back.
- **The plan's standing rules are the coach's.** The coach writes them into `plan-data.json` with the plan and revises them at the check-in: `rules` (calibration, light and test weeks, what a low-readiness day or a sore muscle changes, missed time, the stall response, Hotel), each logged lift's `progression` (double, linear, hold, or a target of the coach's) and `express.short`. Pahlavan does each rule's arithmetic and starts every target's reason with it ("The plan's rule: …"); a rule the plan doesn't set keeps the old default, said as "Pahlavan's default", with one `legacy-default` note. A plan that sets no rules gets the same numbers as before.
- **Light weeks come from the plan.** Every screen reads one light-week calendar: every 4th week unless `rules.lightWeeks` says otherwise (every N weeks, or the weeks listed, with their sets, load and RIR), and a mesocycle ends at each light week. An early signal (low readiness with a falling e1RM, stalls, poor recovery) is a fact for the coach unless the rule says to act on it; the coach moves a light week by editing the plan. `review` adds `deload.signals`, `week.mesocycleWeeks` and `rules`.
- **A lift's step and its stalls are the coach's.** Under a lift's own rule an increase over the load-jump guide (about 10 % a week, a rough guide) is reported, not capped; a coach-set target holds once met; with `rules.stall.response: "report"` a stalled lift gets the facts (`lifts[].stallFacts`) and the coach picks the response.
- **The Plan screen** gains a **Standing rules** card (each rule in words, marked "default: your coach hasn't set this" until the coach does), and its phase targets are the ones your coach set in the profile.
- **`engine evidence`: the rules as data.** Each rule with its number (read from the constant the engine checks with), its grade, a note and its sources: volume, effort, load, rest, tempo, order, range of motion, progression, light weeks, the calibration week, cardio, activity, the regions, how a finding is confirmed, and each standing rule with its default. `--topic` gives one part; `check`'s grades come from the same rules.
- **`engine catalogue`: search the movements** by pattern, region (by name too, "side delts"; an unsupported one such as "inner chest" searches its nearest option and says why), muscle, the equipment at hand and text, each with its muscles, regions, abdominal-strain tag, level and easier and harder versions. The order is for display, never a choice; the version that loads a two-joint muscle long (the seated leg curl, overhead triceps extensions, standing calf raises) comes first. A movement the catalogue doesn't hold counts like one in it once its guide entry names its muscles.
- **Muscle regions.** The engine knows the parts of a muscle that training studies show growing differently with the exercise: the quads' rectus femoris and vasti, the hamstrings at the hip and at the knee, the gastrocnemius and soleus, the triceps long head, the front and side delts, the upper chest, and as rough guides the rear delts, the upper traps and the mid-back, each with its grade and sources. Catalogue movements say which region they bias, and the profile can declare `priorityRegions`. Popular ideas the evidence doesn't support (the inner chest, the VMO, upper lats, the biceps peak, upper glutes) are warned about with the nearest supported option (`unsupported-region`).
- **Sets per region, and region gaps.** A set counts for each region its movement biases (1 when the region's muscle is a main muscle, half when it helps); your sets per muscle don't change. `review` reports every region planned and done (`volume.regions`). `check` warns `region-coverage` when a priority muscle's region gets under 4 direct sets a week and `region-declared-short` when a region you declared weak gets under 6 near failure, each with up to 5 catalogue movements that fit your equipment, Ledger, dislikes and confirmed findings; both are rough guides an override can answer.
- **Declared weak regions, followed through.** A region you declare weak makes its muscle a priority muscle everywhere: `review`'s bands, `check`'s 10–16, and the Progress and Plan screens (it was a priority for the region rules only). This week and the last 4 weeks now judge a declared region's sets by effort: a set logged at 4 or more reps in reserve, or a ladder level under yours that day, no longer counts toward its 6 near failure, and a set logged without RIR takes the plan item's. A region short of its aim that nothing in the catalogue can cover with your equipment shows "No option" instead of "Under". The options `check` lists leave out what a confirmed finding rules out (no overhead presses for the side delts with a confirmed limited overhead reach). `catalogue --equipment` leaves a word it doesn't know out with a warning instead of stopping. The pec deck is a chest fly, a pattern of its own, so the plan builder never makes it a main lift; the slider leg curl is an intermediate movement (a beginner's plan keeps the glute bridge).
- **Regions on the screens.** On Progress, hovering a muscle splits its sets into direct sets and half credit from helping lifts and lists its regions; a **Regions** list under the body map shows each region with an aim as **Under** or **OK**, your declared ones starred, the unmeasured ones marked "rough guide". The Plan's Sets per muscle gives each declared region its own line ("Side delts: 6 direct, aim 6").
- **Nine more movements, 100 in all:** the lying leg curl, a slider leg curl, the seated calf raise, a band lateral raise, the reverse pec deck, the cable overhead triceps extension, the pec deck, the hip abduction machine and the dumbbell shrug, each with its muscles, region, strain tag, level and easier and harder options. "Lying Leg Curl" in a plan now reads as the lying curl, and their common names are recognised.
- **Findings ask before they act.** A body-assessment row or a self-check result that would change exercise choice (a rib flare on the overhead reach, a rounded hinge, balance under 30 s) is a question for you first, never a diagnosis, and the profile records your answer (`emphasis[].confirmed`, `selfCheck.confirmed.<test>`). Confirmed, it applies as before; declined, only its warm-up lines stay, so overhead presses come back when you press overhead without trouble; not answered yet, it ranks the regression first and rules nothing out. A confirmed lagging area makes its muscle a priority muscle in every count (`check`, `review`, the screens), as it always did in the plan builder. A profile from before has no answers: its plan is unchanged and `plan` and `review` warn `finding-unconfirmed` with the question to ask.
- **Plan items have `rir` and `timed` fields,** read before the note: `rir` a number or `[lo, hi]`, `timed` a timed item's minutes and, for intervals, rounds, work, rest, warm-up and cool-down. Plans without them read as before.
- **Intervals count as intervals.** A timed item's sets are its rounds, so a 22-minute item no longer shows as 11 sets; Today, the Log panel, the Plan screen and a copied plan say the structure in words, and a session's length counts the warm-up, rounds, easy minutes and cool-down. In the calibration week intervals are easy steady minutes, with no week-1 line needed in the note.
- **A plan export keeps your names and reasons.** Exported over your plan file (`plan --format plan-data --vault .`), a movement your plan, your `lifts` or your log already names keeps that name (the catalogue's "One-arm dumbbell row" stays your "One-Arm DB Row"), with its history, lifts entry and guide text; a logged lift keeps its name even when its guide entry keeps its own tags (an override, which then counts: `muscle override` says so); an item unchanged in its session keeps its "Why:" line; every item without one is listed (`why lines`) for your coach to write, and so are the form cues and temporary changes of the notes as written that the export leaves out (`note cues`, `temporary changes`). The export writes the calibration, light and test weeks and each lift's rule as standing rules.
- **Your plan starts on the day you chose.** `plan-data.json` takes an optional `start` (a date): the Monday-to-Sunday week holding it is plan week 1 on every screen and in the coach from the day the plan is written, so the plan's weeks and dates agree from the start (they counted from the current week until a session was logged). The coach writes it with a fresh start's or a reset's plan; the next month, a phase review's next block and every other change keep it, so week 9 follows week 8 (`check` and `plan-write` warn `start-changed` on a draft that drops or moves it, and an export over your plan carries it). `plan --start D` starts the generator on the week holding D and writes it. A plan without one counts as before. Before the start week, Today shows no session and says when your plan starts.
- **A first plan works on a fresh vault.** With no plan written yet (right after the intake), `plan --format plan-data --vault .` exports a fresh plan for the coach to start its drafts from, with a `no-plan` note, and `review --plan`, `session --plan`, `check --plan` and `plan-write` work on the draft. `review`, `readiness`, `session`, `tracked`, `due`, `habits`, `food`, the check-in template and the day answer with a `no-plan` warning instead of stopping; `check` with no plan says there is nothing to check; what needs the plan (picking a session, a lift never logged, a ladder level, a fix) says `no-plan`; `log add` takes a custom session. Without a plan there are no plan weeks, phases or mesocycles.
- **Too many priority muscles, said.** `check` warns `priority-room` (a rough guide) when your priority muscles (the profile's, your declared weak regions' and confirmed lagging areas) are more than your week has room for: 3 with 4 or more lifting days, 2 with 3, 1 with 2 or 30-minute sessions. A priority trained only on the short days doesn't count; one with direct sets on a lifting day does. The plan builder's `priority-over-room` now counts the same muscles the same way. The coach asks which come first and keeps the rest under Later priorities, or keeps them all with an override and its reason; at the Sunday check-in it says so once, then leaves it for the phase review.
- **The coach asks before it proposes.** The plan job asks its open questions in one message (each finding you haven't answered, priority muscles past your week's room, the start date, and "picture week 6: the plan has failed, why?") and waits for your answers before it designs; then the options, the proposal and your yes, then the writes. It runs each engine command on its own, so you see every one, and reads the evidence library by topic.
- **Smaller fixes.** The Plan screen names the Ledger's ruled-out movements and marks one Pahlavan doesn't recognise; a session logged without its exercise list is counted from today's plan and marked approximate (`snapshot-rebuilt`); engine text names its own `log ladder` command, never a script only one vault had; the generator meets each major muscle's minimum of 4 sets when it can.
- **For developers:** `tools/eval-plans.mjs` scores coach-written draft plans for the made-up test athletes (hard errors, warnings, overrides, priority muscles in band, region rules met, session minutes, pull to push), so models and prompts can be compared. `engine --version --json` gives `facts` (the calibration RIR, the volume tiers, the default light week, the region rules, plan-data's keys and every `unexpressible` kind) from the engine's constants; the skill's self-check fails a ship whose docs contradict them, quote a field as missing that exists, keep a Python snippet the engine replaced, or whose engine output names a vault-only script, and `npm run ship-skill` runs `engine check` on the test vault first. The self-check passes on a vault with no plan yet: its draft runs read a fresh export, and the commands that need the plan are held to their documented `no-plan` error. `tools/terminal-svg.mjs --transcript` reads a Claude Code session file (`.jsonl`) as JSON, counting only the main session's assistant text (not a subagent's), so a coach line with quotes checks against it; a `.json` export holding the events is read the same way and any other JSON is refused with a message; a plain-text transcript works as before.

## 0.25.0: the first public beta

The first public version of Pahlavan, free while it's in beta. It was built and used in private before this, from 0.1 to 0.24.

- **The plugin:** Today (the session, how to do each movement and why it's in your plan, a checklist, the log), Progress (your week, strength over time, the body record and photos, your habits with 4-week charts for sitting, stand-ups and desk breaks), Plan, Check-in, Food, and reminder cards with quiet hours.
- **The coach:** the `/fitness-coach` skill for Claude Code (intake, plan, logging, the Sunday check-in and letter, reviews, progress reports, an eating plan, habits), set up from Pahlavan's welcome card or its settings.
- **Licence:** the Elastic License 2.0. The code is open to read, use and change; it can't be offered as a service, have a licence key removed or got around, or lose its notices.
- **Calls are not detected:** a stand-up or sitting count treats a call without typing as time away. Pausing during calls, through a small helper program you choose in the settings, is planned for a later version.
