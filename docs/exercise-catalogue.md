## Squat

### Box squat (bodyweight, sit back to a box)

Beginner · no equipment · bodyweight · abdominal strain low

- **Muscles:** quads, glutes
- **Regions:** vasti
- **Easier:** Supported squat (holding a door frame or rail). **Harder:** Bodyweight squat, Goblet squat, Heel-elevated goblet squat, Leg press.
- **Setup:** Stand in front of a box or chair, feet shoulder width; a higher box makes it easier.
- **Do:** Sit back until you touch the box lightly, then stand up.
- **Avoid:** Dropping onto the box, or rocking forward to get up.
- **Also called:** chair squat, sit to stand, chair stand
- **Suits:** warm up, balance · fits a hotel room

### Bodyweight squat

Beginner · no equipment · bodyweight · abdominal strain low

- **Muscles:** quads, glutes; helping: abs
- **Regions:** vasti
- **Easier:** Box squat (bodyweight, sit back to a box), Supported squat (holding a door frame or rail). **Harder:** Goblet squat, Box pistol (one leg, down to a box), Assisted pistol (one leg, holding a post or rings), Snap-down (fast drop to a landing).
- **Setup:** Feet about shoulder width, toes turned out a little.
- **Do:** Sit down between the heels, chest proud, knees following the toes, then stand.
- **Avoid:** Knees caving in, or the heels lifting.
- **Also called:** air squat
- **Asks of the body:** hip deep flexion, knee deep flexion · **Suits:** warm up · fits a hotel room

## Skills

### Chest-to-wall handstand hold

Intermediate · no equipment · a hold for time · abdominal strain low

- **Muscles:** shoulders; helping: triceps, abs
- **Easier:** Pike push-up, Wall walk (walk the feet up the wall), Pike hold on a box (feet up, hips over the hands). **Harder:** Wall handstand push-up, Handstand kick-up and bail.

## What each demand means

- **neck loaded:** Load goes through the head or neck (handstand push-ups, dragon flags).
- **shoulder overhead:** The arms work above the head under load: presses, overhead holds and carries, pulldowns and pull-ups.
- **shoulder hang:** Body weight hangs from the hands (hangs, pull-ups, hanging raises, levers).
- **shoulder extension:** The upper arm is pulled far behind the body under load (dips, bench dips, muscle-ups, skin the cat).
- **wrist extension:** Body weight rests on bent-back wrists (hands-down push-ups and planks, handstands, crawling, the front rack).
- **grip heavy:** Heavy or long gripping (deadlifts, heavy rows, carries, hangs), which also loads the elbows and forearms.
- **spine axial:** A heavy load rests on the shoulders or overhead and presses down through the spine.
- **spine flexion:** The spine curls forward repeatedly or under load (crunches and other curl-ups).
- **spine extension:** The lower back arches against load or gravity (back extensions, prone lifts).
- **spine rotation:** The trunk turns against resistance (chops and other loaded rotation).
- **back bent over:** The torso is tipped forward and unsupported while holding a load (hinges, bent-over rows and flies).
- **hip deep flexion:** The hip bends deeply under load (deep squats, pistols, a deep leg press).
- **knee deep flexion:** The knee bends well past a right angle under load (deep squats, pistols, hack squat, leg press, cossacks).
- **kneeling:** Weight rests on a knee (half-kneeling work, kneeling stretches, Nordics).
- **impact:** Jumping, hopping, running or landing.
- **inversion:** The head goes below the hips or the heart (handstands, pike holds, skin the cat).
- **vigorous:** Vigorous effort, about 6 METs or more for most adults (intervals, running, skipping, burpees, swings, sled pushes).
- **floor:** Getting down to the floor and back up.
- **balance:** A real balance challenge without support (single-leg standing work, arm balances, an unstable base).
- **supine:** Lying on the back for the set.
- **prone:** Lying face down for the set.

And the roles a movement can suit:

- **warm up:** Suits the warm-up before a session.
- **cool down:** Suits the easy end of a session.
- **posture:** Part of the posture emphasis (upper back, neck, shoulders, chest opening).
- **balance:** Balance training, for example the balance work every session at 65 and over.
- **desk break:** Works standing or seated in work clothes during a desk break.
